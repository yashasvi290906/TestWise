Algorithmic Trade-offs

1. Fixed Order

Rigidly follows a predefined canonical sequence regardless of observed evidence. Diagnostic blind spot: wastes resources performing redundant tests after high certainty is already achieved.

2. Cheapest First

Greedily selects the lowest-cost investigation available. Conserves financial budget in the short term, but frequently selects uninformative tests that fail to resolve diagnostic ambiguity.

3. Greedy Information Gain

Computes 1-step Shannon Expected Information Gain per unit cost (EIG / Cost). Highly effective myopic heuristic, but susceptible to "dead-end" traps where an initial low-yield test is required to unlock high-yield contingent tests.

4. Contingent Optimal (Bellman)

Full recursive value iteration over the belief simplex within budget constraints. Finds the globally optimal decision tree where downstream actions genuinely depend on upstream observed outcomes.
